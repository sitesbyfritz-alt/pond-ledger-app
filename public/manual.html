1. What PondLedger does

PondLedger answers one simple question at any moment: “What is a kilogram of fish costing me in this pond, right now?”

Many farmers only find out if a pond made money after they sell — and by then it is too late to change anything. PondLedger works out your cost while the fish are still growing, so you can act early: feed smarter, fix a sick pond, or pick the best week to sell.

You do one small job each day — write down how much feed you gave. The app quietly does all the maths behind the scenes: your cost per kilo, how efficiently feed turns into fish, how many fish are surviving, and whether you are ahead of or behind today's market price.

Your data is yours Everything is stored only on this phone. There is no account and nothing is sent to the internet (except the optional AI summary, which you turn on yourself). If you clear the app or lose the phone without a backup, the records are gone — so please read the Backup section.

2. Install it on your phone

PondLedger opens in your web browser, but you can add it to your home screen so it opens like a normal app and works even with no network at the pond side.

On Android (Chrome)

  1. Open PondLedger in Chrome.
  2. Tap the three dots ⋮ at the top-right.
  3. Tap “Add to Home screen” (or “Install app”), then Add.
  4. A PondLedger icon appears on your home screen. Open it from there from now on.

On iPhone (Safari)

  1. Open PondLedger in Safari.
  2. Tap the Share button (the square with an arrow going up).
  3. Scroll down and tap “Add to Home Screen”, then Add.
Tip After installing once, you can use PondLedger with no internet — perfect for logging feed right at the pond. Your entries save on the phone and are always there when you reopen the app.

4. Reading the Dashboard

The Dashboard (the home screen) is your whole farm at a glance. Across the top are four big numbers. Here is what each one means in plain terms:

NumberWhat it tells you
Blended cost / kgYour break-even price across all ponds together — what one kilo of fish has cost you so far. Sell above this and you profit; sell below it and you lose.
Total biomassThe total weight of live fish you are holding right now, across every pond (in kilograms).
Cash in feedHow much money you have put into feed so far. This is money working in your ponds, not yet returned.
Margin vs marketThe gap between today's market price and your cost. Green (a “+”) means you are making money per kilo; red (a “−”) means a kilo currently costs more than it sells for.

Below the numbers you'll find:

  • Cost / kg trend — a line showing how your cost per kilo changes over the cycle. It should fall as fish grow into their feed.
  • Needs attention — short alerts, like a pond nearing harvest weight, a pond that still needs a weight sample, or an unusual jump in fish deaths.
  • Pond cards — one card per active pond showing its day number, cost/kg, FCR, weight held, and survival.
If a number shows “—” A dash means there isn't enough information yet. Cost per kilo and FCR need at least one weight sample — weigh a few fish and the numbers appear.

5. The daily feeding log

This is the one habit that makes everything else work. It takes under 30 seconds.

  1. Tap the round + button at the bottom of the screen (or “Log feeding”).
  2. If you have more than one active pond, tap the pond you fed.
  3. Type how many kilos of feed you gave today.
  4. The cost fills in by itself from your feed price. If today's feed cost was different, just type over it.
  5. Tap Save log. You'll see “Saved on this device ✓”.
Tip · repeat yesterday Fed the same amount as last time? Tap “Repeat yesterday” and it fills the number in one touch. Two taps and you're done.

On the same screen you can also, when needed, tap to Record deaths or Add a weight sample — covered next.

Reminders appear at the top of the log screen — for example, if a pond hasn't been fed in a while, or is due for a weigh-in.

6. Ponds & batches (cycles)

A pond is a physical pond, tank, or tarpaulin. A batch (the app calls it a cycle) is one stocking of fish in that pond, from the day you put fingerlings in until you harvest and close it.

Add a pond

  1. Go to Ponds and tap to add a new pond.
  2. Give it a name (e.g. “Pond A”) and pick the type (earthen, concrete, tarpaulin, or tank).

Stock a batch

Open the pond and stock a cycle. You enter:

  • How many fingerlings you put in (e.g. 500).
  • What each fingerling cost (e.g. ₦50).
  • Their starting weight (about 5 g is typical) and your target weight (e.g. 1,000 g = 1 kg).
  • The stocking date — the day they went in.

From then on, the pond keeps its own running story: cost/kg, FCR, survival, and days in cycle. When you finish selling, you close the cycle and can stock a fresh batch.

Why the starting details matter Fingerling count and cost are part of your true cost. Getting them right at stocking means every number afterwards is honest — no guessing at harvest time.

7. Weighing your fish

Weight samples are how the app knows how much fish you're holding and what a kilo really costs. Without them, cost/kg and FCR can't be calculated (they show “—”).

  1. Scoop out about 10 fish at random.
  2. Weigh them together and work out the average weight of one fish in grams (total grams ÷ number of fish).
  3. In the daily log, tap “Add weight sample” and enter that average (e.g. 450 g).

Do this every week or two. The more samples you add, the more accurate your cost trend and harvest timing become.

Tip Weigh at the same time of day (e.g. before the morning feed) for steadier readings. A handful of fish is enough — you're after a fair average, not every fish.

8. Recording deaths

When you lose fish, record it — it keeps your survival rate and your true cost honest (fewer fish means the same feed cost is spread over less fish).

  1. In the daily log, tap “Record deaths”.
  2. Enter how many fish you lost that day. Save as normal.

If deaths suddenly spike, the Dashboard raises a “mortality spike” alert so you can check water, feed, or disease quickly.

9. Feed & market price

Two prices power all your money figures. Set them in Settings ▸ Feed & market.

  • Feed price (₦ per kg) — what you pay for feed. This auto-fills your daily feed cost so you rarely type it by hand.
  • Market price (₦ per kg) — what a kilo of table-size fish sells for today. This is what “Margin vs market” compares your cost against.
Keep the market price current Prices move. Update the market price whenever it changes, otherwise “Margin vs market” and the profit view will be comparing against an old number.

10. Analytics & trends

The Analytics screen is where you study a pond in depth and make decisions:

  • Cost-per-kg trend — watch it fall as fish grow. A rising line is a warning that feeding or deaths need attention.
  • Break-even — the price you must beat to profit.
  • Profit view — what you'd make at today's market price.
  • On-device insights — plain bullet points the app works out for you, with no internet needed.
A quick worked example Say a pond has cost you ₦900,000 so far (fingerlings + feed + other costs) and is holding 1,000 kg of fish. Your cost per kilo is ₦900,000 ÷ 1,000 = ₦900/kg. If the market pays ₦1,500/kg, your margin is +₦600 per kilo. Grow the fish a little more and that cost per kilo usually keeps falling.

11. The AI summary (optional)

On the Analytics screen you can tap “Generate summary” to get a short, plain-English read on how your ponds are doing — like a helper reading your numbers aloud and pointing out what stands out.

It is completely optional and stays off until you turn it on. When you use it:

  • Only farm totals (like cost/kg and weights) are sent to work out the summary — never your name, unless you choose to include it.
  • The app's own on-device bullet points always stay, with or without the AI summary.
  • If there's no internet, the summary simply won't run — everything else still works.
Tip Treat the AI summary as a friendly second opinion, not gospel. Your logged numbers are the truth; the summary just helps you notice things faster.

12. Deciding when to harvest

There's usually a sweet spot: hold too short and fish are small and cost more per kilo; hold too long and extra feed stops paying for itself. PondLedger helps you spot it:

  • Watch the cost / kg trend flatten out — when it stops falling much, extra feeding is giving less back.
  • The Dashboard flags a pond “nearing harvest weight” once the average reaches about 85% of your target.
  • Check margin vs market — harvest while the gap is healthy.

13. Backup & restore — please do this

Because your records live only on this phone, a backup is your safety net. It's one tap.

Make a backup

  1. Go to Settings ▸ Backup & restore.
  2. Tap “Export backup”. A file downloads (named like pondledger-backup-2026-08-11.json).
  3. Keep it somewhere safe — email it to yourself, or save to Google Drive / your files.

Restore (or move to a new phone)

  1. Open PondLedger on the phone, go to Settings ▸ Backup & restore.
  2. Tap “Choose file to import” and pick your backup file.
  3. Choose how to bring it in:
    • Merge — adds the backup's records to what's already there.
    • Replace all — wipes this phone's data first, then loads the backup.
  4. Confirm. Done.
Handle “Replace all” with care “Replace all” erases the current data on this phone before importing. Only use it when you're sure the backup file is the one you want. If unsure, choose Merge.
Tip · a good routine Export a backup at the end of each week, and always before changing phones or clearing your browser.

14. Locking the app with a PIN

Add a PIN so others can't open your books on your phone.

  1. Go to Settings ▸ App lock.
  2. Enter a 4–8 digit PIN twice and enable it.
  3. From now on, PondLedger asks for the PIN when you open it.

To remove or change it, enter your current PIN in the same place. To change it, remove the old one and set a new one.

Important The PIN is a screen lock, not encryption — it stops casual snooping, but your data still sits unencrypted in the phone's storage. And there's no “forgot PIN” reset, so don't forget it.

15. Word list

Cost per kilo (cost/kg)
All money spent on a pond so far, divided by the weight of live fish it's holding. Your true cost to produce one kilo.
Break-even
The price you must sell at just to cover costs — the same as your cost per kilo. Above it you profit; below it you lose.
Biomass
The total weight of live fish you're holding, in kilograms.
FCR (Feed Conversion Ratio)
How many kilos of feed it took to add one kilo of fish. Lower is better — around 1.0–1.5 is strong for catfish. A high FCR means feed is being wasted.
Survival rate
The share of stocked fish still alive, as a percentage.
Fingerling
A young fish you stock the pond with.
Cycle / batch
One stocking of a pond, from putting fish in to harvesting and closing it.
Margin vs market
Today's market price minus your cost per kilo — your profit (or loss) on each kilo right now.
Weight sample
The average weight of a handful of fish, used to estimate the whole pond.

16. Common questions

Do I need internet to use it?

No. Once installed, PondLedger works fully offline. The only feature that uses the internet is the optional AI summary, which you turn on yourself.

Do I need to create an account or pay?

No account, no sign-in, no fee. Everything runs on your phone.

Why does cost per kilo show a dash “—”?

It needs at least one weight sample. Weigh about 10 fish, add the average in the daily log, and the number appears.

Can I track more than one pond?

Yes — add as many ponds as you like. The Dashboard blends them into farm totals, and each pond keeps its own figures.

What if I miss a day of logging?

No problem. Log it when you can and set the correct date on the entry. More regular logging just makes the numbers more accurate.

Will an app update delete my records?

No. Updates don't touch your data, and imports of older backups are safely upgraded. Still, keep a backup — it's your safety net against a lost or reset phone.

Can two people use it on different phones?

Each phone keeps its own records. To move data between phones, export a backup on one and import it on the other. (Live multi-phone sync isn't in this version.)
